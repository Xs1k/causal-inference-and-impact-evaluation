Course Overview

The course covers the design, planning, and coordination of logistics systems and supply networks. It combines a managerial view of supply chain strategy with the quantitative models used to support decisions on network configuration, production planning, inventory, transportation, sourcing, and forecasting.

Structure: eight topics, moving from the strategic level (network design) through tactical planning (aggregate planning, inventory, transportation) to coordination across independent supply chain actors.

1. Introduction to Logistics and Supply Chain Management

  • The complexity of the supply network
  • Objectives and sources of uncertainty
  • Cycle view and push/pull view of the supply chain
  • Key supply chain drivers and performance metrics: facilities, inventory, transportation, information, sourcing, and pricing

2. Distribution Network Design

  • Factors influencing network configuration
  • A structured framework for network design decisions
  • Models for supply network design and facility location

3. Aggregate Planning

  • Definition and objectives
  • Planning strategies
  • Linear programming models
  • Development of a rough-cut master production schedule

4. Inventory Management

Economies of scale

  • EOQ model
  • Order aggregation
  • Discount policies

Managing uncertainty

  • Safety stock
  • Periodic vs. continuous review
  • Risk pooling

5. Transportation Planning

  • Transport modes and their characteristics
  • Key trade-offs and decision criteria
  • Optimization models for transportation problems

6. Sourcing and Supplier Selection

  • Make-or-buy decisions
  • Multicriteria approaches for supplier evaluation and selection

7. Demand Forecasting

  • Forecast components and methods
  • Time series forecasting techniques

8. Supply Chain Coordination

  • The value of information sharing
  • The bullwhip effect
  • Barriers to coordination and managerial levers to enhance collaboration across the supply chain
